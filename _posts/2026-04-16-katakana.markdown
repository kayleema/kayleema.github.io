## ①	The Dakuten: When katakana are written with extra "tick marks"（゛）	

Voicing Mark.								

✨	Changes a sound where you don't vibrate vocal cords to one where you do (unvoiced⇨voiced)								

Changes k⇨g   s⇨z    t⇨d　h⇨b								

<table>
<tr><td>ガギグゲゴ</td>			<td>pronounced ga gi gu ge go</td></tr>					
<tr><td>ザジズゼゾ</td>			<td>pronounced za ji zu ze zo (ji is special case)</td></tr>					
<tr><td>ダヂヅデド</td>			<td>pronounced da ji zu de do (ji and zu are special cases)</td></tr>					
<tr><td>バビブベボ</td>			<td>pronounced ba bi bu be bo (bu is special case)</td></tr>					
<tr><td>ヴ</td>			<td>(special case pronounced as v)</td></tr>
</table>

## ②	The HanDakuten: When katakana are written with an extra little circle（゜）									

✨	Changes h to p								

<table>
<tr><td>パピプペポ</td>			<td>pronounced pa pi pu pe po</td></tr>
</table>

## ③	When katakana ヤ、ユ、ヨ is written small									

✨	drop consonant of preceding kana								

<table>
<tr><td>キャ、キュ、キョ</td>			<td>pronounced kya, kyu, kyo</td></tr>					
<tr><td>シャ、シュ、ショ</td>			<td>pronounced sha, shu, sho</td></tr>					
<tr><td>リャ、リュ、リョ</td>			<td>pronounced rya, ryu, ryo</td></tr>					
<tr><td>チャ、チュ、チョ</td>			<td>pronounced cha che cho</td></tr>
</table>

## ④	When katakana vowels ア、イ、ウ、エ、オ are written small									

✨	drop vowel of preceding kana								

<table>
<tr><td>ファフィフェフォ</td>			<td>pronounced fa fi fe fo</td></tr>					
<tr><td>チェ</td>			<td>pronounced che</td></tr>					
<tr><td>ジェ</td>			<td>pronounced je</td></tr>
</table>

## ⑤	When ツ is written small									

✨	Pronounced as a "glottal stop". Like the time your breath stops in uh'oh or the native pronunciation of Hawa'ii								

(note that in english romanization this is indicated by a duplicated consonant)

## ⚠︎	Note that ツシソンリ are easy to mix up									

due to visual similarity

</table>
